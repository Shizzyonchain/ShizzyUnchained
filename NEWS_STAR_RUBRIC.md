# News star ratings

Owner correction November 9, 2026. Read before every daily edition and three-hour monitor. This is the consolidated priority scale; the owner's latest explicit correction takes precedence over older examples.

| Stars | Editorial significance |
| --- | --- |
| 1 | Small useful housekeeping or a weak early watchlist item. Exclude empty promotion rather than padding the report. |
| 2 | Minor changes, maintenance and bounded early plans with limited consequences. A verified team name change does not belong here. |
| 3 | Meaningful operational or participation updates of limited scope. Routine competitions are capped here; Shizzy videos/interviews/livestreams receive three. |
| 4 | Major subnet or ecosystem news. Verified team/project name changes receive at least four, even without a product release, price reaction or disclosed sale. Substantive products, adoption, research, protocol/security developments and verified changes of operating team should be assessed here. Substantive non-video Shizzy coverage retains its existing minimum-four priority. |
| 5 | Exceptional significance beyond an ordinary major story, supported by concrete evidence and a specific explanation. A rename is not automatically five. |

Stars measure importance to readers. Verification status separately describes what is established. A team-reported plan must remain a plan even if important. Do not lower a verified rename merely because buyer identity, a sale price or a product launch is unknown. Do not inflate a routine hotkey rotation, scheduled coldkey swap, URL/logo/contact edit or spelling correction into a takeover or rebrand. Establish which kind of change occurred and explain it.

For every selected item, record what changed, who is affected, why the rating fits and what remains unverified. Price can support attention and priority when a dated, currency-consistent reaction is measured; price alone cannot establish the story or supply a fixed rating bump. Preserve competition/video exceptions.

## Mandatory daily identity and team-change check

Use the configured owner node first, with a recorded archive fallback if required. Inspect the full finalized interval since the last lifecycle review, including identity set/removal, registration/removal, owner/coldkey and owner-hotkey events, including changes reversed between snapshots. Verify execution, block/hash, time, extrinsic/event and exact before/after identity, owner and registration. Equal endpoint snapshots are not proof of no events.

Join each verified change to the source roster and the edition: old name, new name, aliases, verified team handle/domain, operating-team evidence, announcement date, publication disposition and headline. A changed name must trigger fresh source discovery and a persistent follow-up until the new team's channels and launch/operating status are reviewed. A coldkey change alone does not prove a different beneficial owner or a negotiated sale.

Every active subnet needs an individual source-review row with actual checked sources/window, alias search, media outcomes and specific gaps. NO_REPOSITORY, an empty X profile, a quiet one-day digest or a roster entry does not mean no news. Review seven-day overlap, follow original sources and retain unresolved leads. An inaccessible required source stays BLOCKED; do not mark the full workflow complete.

Before sign-off, reconcile every lifecycle event and unresolved transition against actual edition headlines. Important missed older news receives a dated catch-up with its original event date. After 5 PM Eastern, queue it for the next full edition. Do not silently rewrite old editions, publish duplicate quotes or use a revised rating alone as a new-news trigger.

The publishing validator checks numeric range and a nonempty rationale. It cannot prove editorial judgment or source coverage. The dated audit must contain human-readable ratingReview and lifecycle/source dispositions; passing validation is not editorial sign-off.

## Claim-specific rating audit, November 11, 2026

Rate the consequence of the actual development, using evidence appropriate to that claim. Independent benchmarks, public model weights, audited revenue and our own product test are not universal requirements for four or five stars. A confirmed partnership needs evidence of the relationship and scope; a performance record needs credible measurement; a chain execution needs finalized evidence. Missing evidence limits the unsupported claim, not every other established fact in the story.

### Consistent examples

| Development | Rating decision |
| --- | --- |
| Routine model added to an existing catalog | Usually 3; availability alone does not establish a partnership. |
| Confirmed consequential launch partnership or substantive commercial integration | Assess at 4; verify the named role and counterparty, not just a logo or model listing. |
| Exceptional strategic partnership, deployment or adoption milestone | Eligible for 5 when specific verified scope explains why it is exceptional. A major-company name alone is insufficient. Neither revenue disclosure nor a benchmark is mandatory if other direct evidence establishes exceptional significance. |
| Verified substantive project rename or operating-team transition | At least 4 under the owner's standing rule; routine key/contact/logo changes do not qualify. |
| Material network activation, security incident or released capability | Assess at 4; 5 when consequences, reach or severity are demonstrably exceptional. A plan may be important, but never describe it as execution. |
| Fund thesis or analytical report | Usually 3; 4 for specific original evidence or analysis with substantial reader consequences. Author prestige, length and optimistic forecasts do not establish importance. 5 requires exceptional substance. |
| Routine competition or Shizzy video | Preserve existing maximum-3 competition rule and exact-3 Shizzy video rule. |

A counterparty's total users, assets or market value are context, not users, customers or revenue of the subnet. Distinguish model hosting, authorized distribution, launch partner, paid customer and strategic agreement explicitly. An attributed team announcement may warrant major coverage while counterparty confirmation remains pending; do not upgrade its verification label.

### Required rating-review record and sign-off

For every selected story, record: story identifier, claim type, concrete change, affected audience, primary evidence, actual scope, uncertainty, selected stars, why the adjacent lower rating understates it and (for 4/5) why the next higher rating does or does not fit. No generic 'no independent benchmark/revenue' rationale unless that missing evidence bears on the central claim.

Compare at least the leading product/adoption/protocol/incident stories against research and commentary before choosing storyOrder. A 4-star story can lead another 4-star story; do not inflate scores to fix placement. Check equivalent stories for equivalent treatment and retain a reason for exceptions. Reconcile headline, body, ratingReason, status and displayed placement. Human editorial review is required; structural validation alone is insufficient.

Historical audit findings are review flags, not automatic re-ratings. Preserve old editions and source evidence; corrections require an explicit dated explanation. Regrading alone does not authorize another X post. Apply the existing 5 PM news cutoff and deduplication rules.

## Active publishing contract

Effective for editions dated November 12, 2026 onward, rubric version `2026-11-11` is mandatory. Each story must include `ratingAssessment` with `rubricVersion`, `claimType`, `consequence`, `evidence`, `scope`, `uncertainty` and `adjacentRatingReason` (all nonempty strings). Use `substantive-identity-change`, `routine-competition` and `shizzy-video` for those exact categories; their standing rating exceptions are enforced. Other claim types should describe the actual development, such as partnership, product, research, security or protocol. For five stars, adjacentRatingReason must explain why four understates the verified significance; for four, explain why three understates it and five is not established. The validator checks record completeness and category exceptions, not truth or editorial judgment. Historical editions remain valid without the new fields.
